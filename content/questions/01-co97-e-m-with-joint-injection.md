# Question
We billed 99214 and 20610 on the same day for an established patient with knee pain. Medicare paid the injection but denied the office visit with CO-97. We didn't add a modifier. What should we do?

# Answer
**Short answer:** a joint injection is a minor procedure (0-day global), and Medicare includes the decision to perform it in the procedure's payment. The E/M is only paid separately when it's a **significant, separately identifiable** service, shown with **modifier 25**.

## When modifier 25 applies
- The provider evaluated another problem (for example, uncontrolled hypertension or a new complaint), or
- The work went well beyond the usual pre-procedure assessment of the knee.

If the visit was only "knee pain, decided to inject," the E/M isn't separately payable and CO-97 is correct.

## How to fix it
1. Read the note. Is there a separate assessment and plan beyond the injection decision?
2. If yes, add modifier 25 to 99214, make sure the diagnosis for the separate problem is linked to the E/M line, and request a reopening (or follow your MAC's correction process).
3. If no, adjust the E/M off. Don't bill the patient.

## Prevent it
- Ask providers to document the separate problem in its own assessment and plan.
- Don't add modifier 25 automatically to every visit with a procedure; payers audit it.
