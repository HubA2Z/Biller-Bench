# Question
A patient saw a different doctor in our group two years ago. Our new physician billed 99203 and the payer denied it (B16). They're a new patient to this doctor. Isn't that a new patient?

# Answer
**Short answer:** not if both doctors have the **exact same specialty and subspecialty** in the **same group**. A patient is "new" only if they haven't had professional services from the physician, **or another physician of the exact same specialty and subspecialty in the same group**, within the **past three years**.

## How to check
1. Look at the patient's visit history across the whole group for the past three years.
2. Compare the specialties: a family medicine doctor and an internal medicine doctor are different specialties; two family medicine doctors are the same.

## How to fix it
- If the rule wasn't met, send a corrected claim with an established patient code (99212–99215) at the level the documentation supports.
- If the earlier visit was with a different specialty, or more than three years ago, appeal with that information.

## Note
The payer goes by the specialty it has on file for each provider, so keep enrollment records accurate.
