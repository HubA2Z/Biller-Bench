# Question
We order labs that Medicare sometimes denies for medical necessity. When must we give the patient an ABN, and how do we show it on the claim?

# Answer
**Short answer:** give an **Advance Beneficiary Notice (ABN)** **before** the service whenever you expect Medicare **not to cover** it as reasonable and necessary. It lets you bill the patient if Medicare denies.

## The modifiers
- **GA:** a valid ABN is on file for a service you expect to be denied.
- **GZ:** you expect a denial but **no** ABN was given (the patient can't be billed).
- **GX:** a **voluntary** ABN for a service Medicare never covers (statutorily excluded).

## Rules for a valid ABN
1. Give it before the service, with enough time for the patient to decide.
2. Explain the specific reason you expect a denial.
3. Let the patient choose the option; staff must not pick it for them.
4. Don't use blanket ABNs for every patient.

## Without a valid ABN
If Medicare denies for medical necessity, the denial is usually **CO-50** (provider liable) and you can't bill the patient.
