# Question
We found several Medicare claims that were never submitted, some more than a year after the date of service. What's the deadline and is there any way to still get paid?

# Answer
**Short answer:** Medicare claims must be filed **within 12 months (one calendar year) of the date of service**. Late claims are denied (CO-29) and you can't bill the patient.

## The exceptions
The deadline can be extended to the end of the sixth calendar month after you were notified, when:
- an error or misrepresentation by Medicare or its contractor caused the delay;
- the patient was made **retroactively entitled** to Medicare;
- the patient was **retroactively disenrolled** from a Medicare Advantage plan or PACE and the plan recovered its payment.

## What to do now
1. Check whether any of these claims fit an exception, and keep the proof.
2. Check whether the claims were actually submitted and rejected at the clearinghouse. A rejected claim was never received, so it doesn't stop the clock, but it tells you where the process broke.
3. Write off the rest as late filing.

## Prevent it
- Run a weekly report of unbilled visits.
- Work clearinghouse rejections daily.
