## What it means

The payer reviewed the service and decided its payment is **included in another procedure it already processed**. It isn't saying the service wasn't performed. It's saying the service isn't paid separately. Medicare often adds remark code **M15** (separately billed services were bundled as parts of the same procedure) or **M80** (not covered when done in the same session or date as a service already processed).

## Common causes

1. **A bundling (NCCI) edit.** Two codes were billed together and one is considered part of the other.
2. **No modifier 25 on a same-day E/M.** An office visit was billed with a minor procedure (0 or 10-day global) without showing the visit was significant and separately identifiable.
3. **No distinct-service modifier.** Two procedures that really were separate (different site, session or practitioner) were billed without 59, XE, XS, XP or XU.
4. **A visit inside a global period.** A follow-up visit during the post-op period is part of the surgical package unless it was unrelated to the surgery.
5. **Processing order.** The main procedure was paid on an earlier claim, so this line now bundles into it.

## How to fix it

1. **Find the paired code.** Check the remittance to see which service this line bundled into, on the same claim or an earlier one.
2. **Look up the code pair in the NCCI procedure-to-procedure (PTP) edits.** The modifier indicator tells you what's possible:

- **0**: no modifier can bypass the edit. The denial stands; adjust it off.
- **1**: a modifier can bypass the edit, but only if the documentation supports it.
- **9**: the edit no longer applies.

3. **If the indicator is 1, read the note.** Does it show a separate site, a separate encounter, a different practitioner, or (for an E/M) a significant, separately identifiable problem beyond the decision to do the procedure?
4. **If it does, correct the claim** with the right modifier: 25 for the E/M, or XE, XS, XP or XU (use 59 only when none of the X modifiers fits). For commercial payers, send a corrected claim. For Medicare, use your MAC's reopening process for a processed claim.
5. **For a visit in a global period** that was unrelated to the surgery, use modifier **24** with a diagnosis that supports a separate problem.
6. **If the documentation doesn't support a separate service**, adjust it off. Don't bill the patient.

## Medicare vs. commercial payers

| | Medicare | Most commercial payers |
|---|---|---|
| Bundling rules | NCCI PTP edits and the NCCI Policy Manual | NCCI plus the payer's own claim-editing software |
| Correcting the claim | Reopening, or a redetermination if records are needed | Corrected claim, or an appeal with records |
| 59 vs. X modifiers | Use XE, XS, XP or XU when one applies; 59 only when none fits | Varies by payer; check its modifier policy |

## Prevent it next time

- Run claims through an NCCI edit check before you submit.
- Ask providers to document a **separate assessment** when they bill an E/M with a procedure on the same day.
- Flag patients who are inside a surgical global period at check-in.
- Keep each major payer's modifier policy in your billing notes.
