## What it means

The payer allowed the service but applied some or all of the allowed amount to the patient's **annual deductible**. Group code **PR** means the patient owes it. It isn't a denial in the usual sense and there's nothing to correct.

## Common causes

1. **Early in the plan year,** when deductibles reset (usually January).
2. **High-deductible health plans (HDHPs),** where most services apply to the deductible.
3. **Out-of-network services,** which often have a separate, higher deductible.

## How to fix it

1. **Check the math:** the deductible amount should come out of the allowed amount, not your full charge.
2. **Post it to patient responsibility** and send a statement.
3. **If the patient has secondary insurance,** send the claim to the secondary payer; it may cover some or all of the deductible.
4. **If you think it applied wrongly** (for example, a preventive service that should be covered in full), call the payer and ask for reprocessing or appeal.

## Medicare vs. commercial payers

| | Medicare Part B | Most commercial payers |
|---|---|---|
| Deductible | One annual Part B deductible, set each year by CMS | Varies by plan; individual and family amounts |
| Preventive services | Many have no deductible | ACA preventive services usually covered without cost sharing in network |

## Prevent it next time

- Check remaining deductible during eligibility verification and collect an estimate at check-in.
- Give patients a good-faith estimate where required.
- Flag HDHP patients in your scheduling system.
