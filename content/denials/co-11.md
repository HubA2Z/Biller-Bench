## What it means

X12 describes code 11 as "the diagnosis is inconsistent with the procedure." The payer's edits compared the ICD-10-CM code linked to the line with the procedure and decided they **don't belong together**.

It's closely related to **CO-50** (not medically necessary). CO-11 points more to a **coding mismatch**; CO-50 points to a coverage decision.

## Common causes

1. **Wrong diagnosis pointer.** The right diagnosis is on the claim but linked to the wrong line.
2. **Unspecified or incomplete codes**, when the payer's policy needs a specific code.
3. **Age or sex edits.** A diagnosis or procedure that doesn't fit the patient's age or sex on file.
4. **Diagnosis not listed** in the payer's policy (or a Medicare LCD/NCD) for that procedure.
5. **Symptom coded instead of the confirmed condition** (or the reverse) when the policy is specific.

## How to fix it

1. **Check the diagnosis pointers first.** It's the most common cause and the quickest fix.
2. **Read the documentation.** Code to the highest specificity it supports.
3. **Check the payer's coverage policy** (or the Medicare LCD/NCD) for the procedure's covered diagnoses.
4. **Check the patient's demographics** if an age or sex edit may be involved.
5. **Send a corrected claim** (commercial) or request a reopening (Medicare) with the right diagnosis and pointer.
6. **If the documentation doesn't support a covered diagnosis,** treat it like a medical-necessity denial: adjust it off, unless a valid advance notice was signed.

## Medicare vs. commercial payers

| | Medicare | Most commercial payers |
|---|---|---|
| Covered diagnoses | LCDs and NCDs | Payer medical policies |
| Correction | Reopening for coding errors | Corrected claim |

## Prevent it next time

- Add pointer checks to your claim scrubber.
- Keep diagnosis lists for high-volume tests and procedures at the order or charge step.
- Ask providers to document the most specific diagnosis.
- Fix demographic errors at registration.
