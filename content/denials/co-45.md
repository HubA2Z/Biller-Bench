## What it means

X12 describes code 45 as the charge exceeding the fee schedule or maximum allowable amount. The payer paid based on its **allowed amount**, and the gap between your **charge** and the allowed amount is adjusted off.

- **CO-45:** contractual. You agreed to the payer's rates (in-network or a Medicare participating provider), so you write it off.
- **PR-45:** the patient owes the difference. This mostly applies when a non-participating provider may bill the patient the excess, within the rules of that payer.

## Common causes

1. **Normal contract pricing.** Your fee schedule is higher than the payer's rate, which is expected.
2. **Multiple-procedure or other pricing reductions** applied by the payer.
3. **An outdated or wrong fee schedule** loaded at the payer.

## How to fix it

1. **Post the CO-45 amount as a contractual adjustment.** Don't bill the patient.
2. **Compare the allowed amount with your contract** for that code and payer. If it's lower than it should be, ask the payer to reprocess with the contracted rate, or appeal.
3. **Check for modifier-driven reductions** (for example, multiple procedures) that may explain a lower allowed amount.

## Medicare vs. commercial payers

| | Medicare | Most commercial payers |
|---|---|---|
| Allowed amount | Physician Fee Schedule for your locality | Contracted rate |
| Billing the difference | Participating providers can't; limits apply to non-participating providers | In-network providers can't |

## Prevent it next time

- Load each payer's fee schedule into your billing system so underpayments stand out.
- Run a monthly underpayment report by payer and code.
