## What it means

X12 describes code 27 as "expenses incurred after coverage terminated." The payer's records show the patient's plan **wasn't active on the date of service**. Medicare may add remark **N30** (patient ineligible for this service).

The group code tells you who's responsible under the payer's rules: **CO-27** or **PR-27**. If the patient simply had no coverage, the visit is generally self-pay under your financial policy, but check your contract first.

## Common causes

1. **The patient changed jobs or plans** and gave you the old card.
2. **Retroactive termination.** The plan ended coverage back to an earlier date (common with lapsed premiums and Marketplace plans), sometimes after it already paid.
3. **The patient moved to a Medicare Advantage or Medicaid managed care plan.**
4. **The wrong member was billed**, such as a parent instead of a dependent.

## How to fix it

1. **Re-verify eligibility** for the exact date of service on the payer portal.
2. **Ask the patient** for current coverage and check other payers (for example, Medicare or your state's Medicaid eligibility tools).
3. **Bill the correct payer** and watch its filing deadline.
4. **If the termination was retroactive and the payer had verified active coverage,** appeal with your eligibility check (date, time, reference number).
5. **If there was no coverage,** bill the patient under your financial policy.

## Medicare vs. commercial payers

| | Medicare | Most commercial payers |
|---|---|---|
| Typical cause | Patient enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan | Job change, lapsed premium, plan change |
| Check | MAC portal or eligibility (HETS) shows MA enrollment | Payer portal or 270/271 eligibility check |

## Prevent it next time

- Verify eligibility before every visit, not only at the first visit.
- Save the eligibility response or reference number.
- Ask "Has your insurance changed?" at every check-in.
- Re-verify all patients in January, when many plans change.
