## What it means

X12 describes code 197 as "precertification/authorization/notification/pre-treatment absent." The payer's policy required **approval or notice before the service**, and it couldn't find one that matched this claim.

A related code, **198**, means the authorization was **exceeded** (for example, more visits than approved). Remark **N54** means the claim doesn't match the authorized services.

## Common causes

1. **No authorization was requested** because staff didn't know the service needed one.
2. **The authorization exists but doesn't match**: different code, provider, location, units or dates.
3. **The authorization number wasn't on the claim.**
4. **The service was done after the authorization expired.**
5. **The patient's plan changed** (for example, to a Medicare Advantage plan) and the new plan has its own requirements.

## How to fix it

1. **Check whether an authorization exists** for that patient, service and date on the payer portal.
2. **If it exists but didn't match,** correct the claim to match the approved code, provider and dates, add the authorization number, and resubmit. If the service really differed from what was approved, ask the payer about updating the authorization.
3. **If none was obtained,** ask the payer whether it allows a **retro authorization**. Some do, within a short window or for urgent cases.
4. **If you have proof** the payer approved the service or said none was needed (reference number, call log, portal screenshot), appeal with it.
5. **If none of these apply,** the denial stands. Adjust it off. Don't bill the patient.

## Medicare vs. commercial payers

| | Original Medicare | Medicare Advantage and commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Prior authorization | Limited to specific programs and services | Common; each plan publishes its own list |
| Fix | Follow the program's process | Retro authorization or appeal per plan rules |

## Prevent it next time

- Keep an up-to-date authorization list for your top payers and services.
- Check authorization at scheduling, not at check-in.
- Put the authorization number, dates and units in the patient's account and on the claim.
- Track authorizations nearing expiry or their visit limit.
