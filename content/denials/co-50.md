## What it means

The payer reviewed the claim against its coverage rules and decided the service **wasn't reasonable and necessary** for the diagnosis given. For Medicare this usually means the diagnosis isn't on the covered list in a **Local Coverage Determination (LCD)** or **National Coverage Determination (NCD)**. Medicare may add remark **N115** (decision based on an LCD).

The group code matters:

- **CO-50**: the provider is liable. You can't bill the patient.
- **PR-50**: the patient is liable, typically because a valid advance notice (for Medicare, an ABN) was signed before the service.

## Common causes

1. **Diagnosis not covered** by the LCD or NCD for that service.
2. **Diagnosis not specific enough**, such as an unspecified code when the policy lists specific ones.
3. **Wrong diagnosis linked** to the line (diagnosis pointer error).
4. **Frequency exceeded**, such as a test repeated sooner than the policy allows.
5. **Documentation doesn't support** the need for the service.

## How to fix it

1. **Pull the coverage policy.** For Medicare, find the LCD or NCD for the code in your MAC's jurisdiction. For commercial payers, use the payer's medical policy.
2. **Compare the diagnosis.** Is the documented condition on the covered list? Is the most specific code used? Is it pointed to the right line?
3. **If it was a coding error**, correct the claim (corrected claim for commercial payers; reopening for Medicare).
4. **If the coding was right and the service was necessary**, appeal with the records. For Medicare, that's a redetermination within 120 days of the initial determination.
5. **If an advance notice was signed**, make sure the claim carried the right modifier (for Medicare, **GA**) so the patient can be billed.
6. **If no notice was signed and the denial is correct**, adjust it off. Don't bill the patient.

## Medicare vs. commercial payers

| | Medicare | Most commercial payers |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage rules | LCDs and NCDs | Payer medical policies |
| Advance notice | ABN, given before the service; modifier GA when an ABN is on file | Varies; some use their own waiver forms |
| Appeal | Redetermination with records, within 120 days | Appeal per payer timelines |

## Prevent it next time

- Build the LCD and NCD diagnosis lists for your high-volume tests and procedures into your order or charge workflow.
- Give an ABN **before** the service whenever you expect Medicare not to cover it. An ABN must be delivered in time for the patient to consider the options, and staff must not pick the option for the patient.
- Code to the highest specificity the documentation supports.
- Audit CO-50 denials by code and diagnosis every month.
